🦎 Just like a chameleon changes color, I build microservices that change their behavior automatically to reach their goals. Let me show you how I do it! 👇

A chameleon does not grow a new skin to go from green to orange: it rearranges tiny crystals it already has under its skin, and in the meantime it keeps walking as if nothing happened! 🦎✨ That is exactly what I am after in the Cloud: a service that changes its behavior without anyone having to stop it to reprogram it. ☁️

Take TeaStore, my small online tea shop, made of several small services that each have their own job. The Recommender service, the one that suggests products to visitors, keeps an eye on its own CPU. As soon as it climbs above 75%, the service understands that it is overheating and makes a decision: "I switch to low power mode". 📈🥵

Easier said than done! 😅 A service in production, with visitors clicking on it, cannot be switched off, modified and switched back on. It has to change color while walking.

This is where today's building block comes in: the adaptation action. In the MAPE-K loop (monitor, analyze, plan, execute), we are at the E, Execute: the moment to act. With AdaptiFlow, an adaptation action is a move the service knows how to make on itself, written once in advance, triggered while it is running. 🔧

In TeaStore, every service has its own:

  • 🧠 Recommender switches to low power mode, then back to normal mode
  • 📦 Persistence turns its cache on or off
  • 🖼️ Image hands its photos over to an external provider
  • 🖥️ WebUI shows or removes its maintenance message

These moves change what the service does, from the inside, like a switch: no restart, no interruption for the visitors. And every action has its own pace: synchronous (the service waits for it to finish) or asynchronous (it launches it and keeps serving). ⚙️

A good part of my work lives right there: with AdaptiFlow, monitoring (the M) and action (the E) have the same standard shape for every service. The "brain" that decides in between can then change without touching the rest: rules today, AI agents tomorrow. 🤖✨

A service is not autonomous because it chooses well, it is autonomous because it can act without pausing. 💡
