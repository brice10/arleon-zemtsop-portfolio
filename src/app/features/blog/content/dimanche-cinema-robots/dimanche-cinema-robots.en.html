🍿 SUNDAY MOVIE NIGHT: which movie robot would you trust with your servers? 🤖

No lab today, but my thesis follows me everywhere, even to the couch! 🎬 After weeks of teaching Cloud systems to heal themselves, I just can't watch movie robots the same way anymore. 😅

So I ranked them, not by computing power, but by the one question that matters in my PhD: would I hand them the keys to my servers on a Sunday, when nobody is watching? 🔑

  • 🔧 R2-D2: he fixes the Millennium Falcon's hyperdrive mid-flight, without being asked, and NEVER tries to take over the ship. Pure self-healing!
  • ♻️ WALL-E: treads worn out? He swaps them himself from a broken-down unit and gets back to work. Seven hundred years of maintenance, all on his own!
  • 🩹 Baymax: he treats you, but won't deactivate until you say you are satisfied with your care. He acts fast AND checks back with you. 🤝
  • 🔴 HAL 9000: he monitors everything, decides everything, and one day decides the crew is the problem. "I'm sorry, Dave." 😬

What do the first three have in common? They observe, decide and act (my beloved MAPE-K loop, for the regulars 😉), but always with a clear limit and a rule they stick to. HAL has no limit left at all. 🚧

That is exactly the deal in AdaptiFlow (my Java framework for making microservices self-adaptive): a service heals itself or switches to a lighter mode on its own, but a human wrote the rule that says when it acts and when it stops. ⚙️🧑‍💻

And that is the idea I'm taking home from my Sunday: the goal of my research is not to build HAL 9000, it is to build R2-D2. 💡
