❄️ Winter is already here… and the next snow alert will show you how my microservices survive a cyberattack! 🛡️

Picture this: tomorrow morning, the weather service puts the whole region under an orange snow alert. 🟠 The alert, by itself, is just a message. It doesn't salt a single road and doesn't close a single school. And yet, in less than an hour, an entire region reorganizes itself! 😮

The city sends out its salt trucks, the bus company shortens its routes, the town hall closes the schools… and the school closing triggers, in turn, the parents' plan (one of whom is about to discover working from home with two kids underfoot 😅). Nobody called everyone one by one: each of them had subscribed to the alert, and each had decided IN ADVANCE what they would do with it. 📋

And this behavior is exactly what I reproduce in the Cloud with microservices! Let me show you how. 🤓☁️

My test store (TeaStore, an online tea shop) has a WebUI service that displays the products to the users: it is the service they interact with from their smartphone, tablet or computer. 📱💻 This service continuously watches the incoming traffic, and when it receives more than 300 requests per second for a whole minute, this is interpreted as a cyberattack meant to choke the service so it can no longer answer in time. 🚨

An event is then triggered, MaliciousTrafficEvent ("malicious traffic"), and WebUI acts as a scout, passing the message on to the other services of the system. In the MAPE-K loop (monitor, analyze, plan, execute), we enter the P, Plan: someone has to decide who does what. And this is where the subscribers come in! 🙋

AdaptiFlow does exactly that: subscribing to an event means writing down in advance what you will do the day it is triggered. A subscriber has three ways to react, and it can combine them: 🛠️

  • 🏠 React at home: Auth (the service that handles logins) temporarily cuts its connections, Recommender switches to low power mode, Image hands the photos over to an external provider. Everyone shovels their own doorstep!
  • 📞 Call another service and ask it to act, like the town hall calling the bus company.
  • 🔗 Trigger another scenario: its reaction is itself an event, with its own subscribers, like the closed school that kicks off the parents' plan.

One alert is then enough to get the whole store moving, each service with its reaction already prepared! 🤝

What I take away from this snow story: the intelligence is not in the alert, it lies with those who listen to it. 💡
