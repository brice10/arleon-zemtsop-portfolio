Context & Mission

As part of my PhD at the Inria laboratories of the University of Lille, my research focuses on the dynamic, secure, and optimised reconfiguration of cloud computing systems. The goal is to design mechanisms enabling cloud infrastructures to reconfigure themselves autonomously in response to load changes, security threats, or optimisation objectives, while guaranteeing service continuity and compliance with defined security policies.

Research Areas

  • Dynamic reconfiguration: Study and design of mechanisms enabling distributed cloud systems to modify their configuration at runtime (topology, resource allocation, request routing) without service interruption.
  • Security through reconfiguration: Integration of security constraints into autonomous decision loops — anomaly detection, isolation of compromised components, real-time update of access control policies.
  • Multi-objective optimisation: Development of reconfiguration strategies arbitrating between performance, cost, energy consumption, and resilience — relying on formal models and tailored heuristics.
  • Experimental validation: Implementation and evaluation of proposed approaches on cloud-native benchmarks (microservices, Kubernetes), building on work carried out during the Inria research engineering contract (AdaptiFlow, Adaptable TeaStore).

Research Engineer

Inria · Université de Lille
📍 Lille, France

Context & Mission

At Inria and the University of Lille, I worked in the field of autonomous systems and cloud computing. My research focused on self-adaptation of distributed systems in the cloud, with the goal of designing mechanisms enabling microservices to autonomously adapt to the dynamic conditions of their environment.

Key Achievements

  • AdaptiFlow: Design and development of a lightweight, extensible Java framework enabling microservices to autonomously adapt to dynamic cloud conditions. The framework relies on abstraction layers for the monitoring and execution phases of the MAPE-K cycle.
  • Adaptable TeaStore: Built a cloud-native benchmark platform dedicated to evaluating self-adaptive capabilities, validating concrete scenarios (database recovery, DDoS mitigation, traffic management).
  • Decentralised event-driven architectures: Engineering of event-driven pipelines to orchestrate the four self-management properties: self-healing, self-protection, self-optimisation, and self-configuration.
  • MAPE-K Abstraction: Development of metrics collectors and adaptation actions for cloud microservices, with systematic workflows minimising intrusion into existing code.
