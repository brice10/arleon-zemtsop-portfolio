🚗 "Are we there yet?": the question that can bring a server down! 😅

Hello everyone! 👋 Every parent knows the scene: you have barely left the parking lot when a small voice from the back seat asks "are we there yet?". Two minutes later, again. Thirty seconds later, again. 🙃 The question is legitimate (the kid REALLY wants to know), but asked every thirty seconds, it costs the driver more than the trip itself! 😅

And guess what: my microservices ask EXACTLY the same question. 🤓☁️

In my test store (TeaStore, an online tea shop made of several small services), the Persistence service is the one that talks to the database. Every 5 seconds, it asks: "is everything okay?". If the answer takes more than 5 seconds to come back, or if the network connection is lost, it understands that the database is down and warns the other services while the database restarts on its own. 🩹

In the MAPE-K loop (monitor, analyze, plan, execute), we are at the M, Monitor. Question number one is WHAT to watch. But right behind it hides the one from the back seat: how often? ⏱️

Because every "is everything okay?" has a price. Every 5 seconds, it costs almost nothing. Every 100 milliseconds, that is 50 times more requests (yes, I did the math 🤓), and the database spends its time answering "yes, yes, I'm fine" instead of serving customers! 💸 Every 5 minutes, it is the opposite: the outage can last long minutes without anyone seeing it, like the kid who fell asleep right before arrival. 😴

With AdaptiFlow, the framework I built, this rhythm is written in the adaptation rule, right next to what is being observed, and each service picks its own way of watching: 👀

  • 🔁 Asking at a regular interval: the only option for the database, because a database that goes down doesn't warn anyone, it just goes silent.
  • 📣 Being told ("I'll tell you when we get there!"): some measurements arrive on their own as soon as they change.
  • 🕐 Looking only when asked ("check the GPS when we leave the highway"): watching for an event can be triggered on demand.

There is no universal right rhythm, only the one that fits what you are watching, and it is written down in advance. 🎯 If I had to keep just one sentence: watching has a price, and when you ask "is everything okay?" too often, the question itself is what slows everything down. 💡
