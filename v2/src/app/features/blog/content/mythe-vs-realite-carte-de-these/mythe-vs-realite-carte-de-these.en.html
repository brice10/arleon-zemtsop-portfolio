🗺️ MYTH vs REALITY: nobody handed me the map of my PhD, and that's a good thing! 🧭

Hey LinkedIn fam! 👋 After about 5 years of code, tickets and project plans in industry, I arrived in research (first as an engineer at Inria, then as a PhD student) with three certainties neatly packed in my suitcase. 😎 All three of them fell, one after the other. 🎳

❌ Myth #1: someone will hand me the roadmap.

In my head, my supervisors would give me a sacred roadmap, I would swear eternal devotion and follow it blindly. The industrial world had sold me that dream! 🗺️ And yet, after one month and twelve days of PhD (yes, I counted 😅), reality was already there: it's MY thesis. My supervisors are guides, a GPS if you like, but the one at the helm is me: they are teaching me to be the captain of the ship ⛵

❌ Myth #2: just read everything before you start.

I opened the firehose of scientific publications… and almost drowned! 🤯 There are so many that you end up wondering what you're even doing there. But very quickly, I flipped the question around: the foundations are laid, and I get to build the walls 🧱✨ And a global army of brains is working on the same questions all over the world: in the end, that's very reassuring! 🌍

❌ Myth #3: it all starts with ONE big breakthrough falling from the sky.

That one was knocked down by Simon Bliudze (my supervisor) during my first months at Inria: big challenges are won with small steps, by cutting every mountain into tasks you can actually finish 🧗‍♂️ AdaptiFlow, my Java framework, was built EXACTLY that way, brick by brick, over five months (with zero visits to the hairdresser 🙈):

  • a metrics collector, so a service can look at itself
  • an evaluator, to say when a measurement becomes abnormal
  • an event, to warn the other services
  • an action, so the service reacts on its own

Taken one by one, none of these blocks is scary. Put end to end, they give you microservices that heal and protect themselves without waiting for a human 🚀

Three myths down, and the same hidden lesson behind each of them: nobody gives you the map, you draw it as you walk 💡 And that is exactly what makes this adventure as dizzying as it is exciting! 🎢
