Happy Friday, everyone ☕ To close the week, I'd like to tell you about a tiny building block, the one that looks at a number and has to say whether it's serious.

Think of a thermometer showing 38 °C, about 100 °F 🌡️ After a run or first thing in the morning, the number is the same and yet nobody draws the same conclusion, because a measurement means nothing until someone interprets it. A system that adapts by itself does the same thing right after monitoring: in the MAPE-K loop, we leave the M and step into the A, the moment where we analyze.

With AdaptiFlow, the self-adaptation framework I'm building during my PhD, that job belongs to the conditional evaluator, and it comes in two kinds. The first one compares a measurement to a threshold, for example "CPU usage goes above 75%". The second one combines several measurements to take the context into account: turning off non-essential features during a traffic peak, but only if the cloud credits are running low.

What I like about this little block is that it forces you to write down in plain terms what you call "abnormal", and I have to admit it's harder than it looks 😄 A jumpy threshold makes the system react over nothing, while one that is too calm lets the real problem slip through.

So what about you: at which number do you decide it's time to act, and who picked it? I'd love to read your answers, and do stick around, I still have a few blocks to open.
