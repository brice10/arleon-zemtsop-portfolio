🥋 A PhD is a combat sport (and I have the belt to prove it)! 😅

Hey network! 👋 Relax, nobody is fighting in the hallways at Inria! 😄 But judo has been part of my life for years (I earned my blue belt right here in France 💙), and the further I go in my PhD, the more I realize the tatami was quietly training me for it. Here are three judo words I carry to the lab. 🥋👇

1️⃣ Ukemi, the art of falling. In judo, the very first thing you learn is how to fall without getting hurt. A PhD works the same way! You set a huge first goal, you MISS it (pretty much everyone does, and that's the whole point 😅), and then you get back up with a better idea. 💡

2️⃣ Randori, the fight nobody loses. It's free practice: no winner, just two partners trying to get better. My PhD randoris are called seminars, winter schools and conferences! I present my work, the questions come flying, and I walk away with the limits of my work right in front of me AND fresh ideas for what comes next. 🤝🚀

3️⃣ Rei, the bow. You bow to your partner before and after every fight, whether you won or ended up flat on your back. In the lab, that means listening before reacting in meetings, and thanking the people who pull my ideas apart, because they are doing me a favor. 🙏 (Self-control, on the other hand, is still a work in progress whenever a cheese platter shows up 🧀😇)

The funny part is that my research tells the very same story! My framework AdaptiFlow gives cloud services that reflex: when the database goes down, they break the fall (one serves what it kept in memory, the interface switches to maintenance mode), the database restarts on its own, and everybody gets back up without a human coming to pick them up. ⚙️☁️

And that's the idea I hold on to, on the mat and in the lab: you learn to fall before you learn to win, whether you are a judoka, a PhD student... or a piece of software! 💡
