A small Thursday confession ☕: there is a sentence in my own research paper that is no longer true, and I have to admit I'm rather proud of it.

This week I reopened the paper we published last year on AdaptiFlow, the self-adaptation framework I'm building during my PhD. I landed on the scenario where the database goes down: the services organize themselves to keep things running and, in black and white, "the system administrator is alerted to restart the database."

I smiled, because today it restarts on its own. The PDF will never know, and I find that quite ironic: I spend my days building systems that reconfigure themselves while they are running, and the document describing them is the only part of the project that cannot update itself 😄

That's when I understood that a paper is a snapshot taken on a given date, while the project keeps moving. And the limitations we write down there look less like a sign of weakness than like the to-do list waiting for us next Monday.

So I'm curious: when you reread something you wrote a year ago, what comes first, the embarrassment or the pride in how far you've come? For me it's a happy mix of both, and I'd love to hear what it is for you. If other lines of that paper start showing their age, this is where I'll come and tell you.
