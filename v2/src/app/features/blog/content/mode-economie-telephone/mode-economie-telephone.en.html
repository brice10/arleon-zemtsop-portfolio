🔋 What if your smartphone understood the Cloud better than you do? 😏

20% battery, two more hours away from home, and there it is, the notification: "Turn on Low Power Mode?" 📱 You tap it without thinking… and yet something brilliant just happened.

Your phone didn't turn off anything essential. It dimmed the screen, paused background email fetching and a few animations, and it keeps doing its job. 👏 It didn't ask for more battery, it simply chose what to let go. And that is exactly the reflex I'm trying to give Cloud systems in my PhD! 🎯

Picture an online store on Black Friday (three weeks away 🛒💸). Visitors pour in, the CPU climbs to 75%, and most systems only have two options: add machines (and pay) or collapse (and lose customers). 😬

AdaptiFlow, the framework I'm building at Inria, adds a third option: every service gets its own low power mode, and it switches it on by itself ⚡

  • 🧠 Recommendations switch to a lighter mode
  • 📦 Data is served from memory (cache)
  • 🖼️ Images are handed over to an external provider

The store keeps selling, nobody pressed a button, and when the load drops back under 60%, everything returns to normal. 🔄 Not at 74%, under 60%! Otherwise the system would spend its whole day switching on and off, like a phone leaving low power mode at 21%. 🤪

This is what we call self-optimization, and here is the idea I wanted to leave you with today: a smart system is not the one with ever more resources, it's the one that knows what to sacrifice when they run short. 💡
